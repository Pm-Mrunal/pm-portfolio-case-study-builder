# Portfolio Strategy Guide

> How to choose which case studies to write, in what order, and how to position them together.
> Reference this with `/tracker` to assess portfolio coverage gaps.

---

## How Many Case Studies Do You Need?

**Minimum viable portfolio: 2 case studies**
One with strong outcomes. One that shows strategic depth (even without hard metrics).

**Strong portfolio: 3-4 case studies**
- 1 shipped product with quantitative results
- 1 complex or ambiguous project showing judgment
- 1 that shows a different PM skill (0-to-1, platform, growth, enterprise — pick what's missing)
- Optional: 1 "learning" case that shows honest self-awareness

**Do not write 6+ case studies of similar type.** Range signals more than depth on the same dimension.

---

## Choosing Which Project to Write First

Write the project that has the best combination of:
1. Strong outcomes (even directional) — something happened that can be described
2. Clear personal ownership — you can say "I owned X" at least a few times
3. Interesting problem — a real problem with real stakes, not a polish project
4. Decision visibility — you made at least one consequential call that could have gone differently

If you have a project with all four: start there.
If no project has all four: pick the one with 2-3 and use the other slots for supplementary cases.

---

## Portfolio Positioning

Think of your portfolio as an argument:
**"Here is the type of PM I am and the type of work I do well."**

Every case study should reinforce the same argument — not contradict it.

Examples:
- Growth PM targeting Series C: all cases should show data fluency, experiment orientation, metric ownership
- 0-to-1 PM targeting early-stage: all cases should show ambiguity tolerance, customer obsession, builder instinct
- Enterprise PM targeting FAANG: cases should show stakeholder complexity, multi-segment thinking, large-scale execution

If a case study contradicts your target positioning, either don't include it or reframe the angle to emphasize the transferable skill.

---

## Case Study Type Coverage Guide

| Type | When to write it | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| Shipped with metrics | You have a live product with data | Execution, outcome orientation, measurement |
| Pre-launch / pilot | Project was cut or validated but not shipped | Judgment under uncertainty, honest scoping |
| 0-to-1 | You built something from nothing | Ambiguity tolerance, problem discovery, builder identity |
| Redesign / iteration | You improved something existing | Diagnosis, prior state understanding, migration thinking |
| Platform / infra | You built something other teams use | Internal customer empathy, scalability thinking |
| AI / technical | You shipped something with significant technical complexity | Technical partnership, scope judgment, tradeoff clarity |
| Learning / pivot | The project failed or was killed | Self-awareness, intellectual honesty, judgment quality |

## The "No Metrics" Problem

Many PMs feel they can't write a case study without quantitative results. This is wrong.

**What you need instead of metrics:**

1. **Validated learning** — "We ran 12 usability sessions and found X, which led us to change Y."
2. **Operational improvement** — "CS ticket volume on this flow dropped from 300/month to 140/month."
3. **Adoption signal** — "Adopted by 7 of our 12 enterprise accounts within the first quarter."
4. **Scope delivered** — "Shipped an MVP that handled X use case, used by N users in the first month."
5. **Strategic impact** — "Established the foundation for a $2M ARR product line that shipped two quarters later."
6. **Honest directional signal** — "Early cohort data suggested activation improved, but we didn't have enough volume to declare a win at the time of this writing."

The bar is not "I have hard numbers." The bar is "I have credible evidence that something happened."

## Signals of a Weak Portfolio

- All case studies are from the same company and same product type
- No case study shows a decision that was difficult or could have gone differently
- Every case study implies success with no reflection
- No case study has clear role ownership (all "we" language)
- Outcomes feel inflated relative to the scope described
- No variation in PM skill — all growth, or all platform, or all 0-to-1

---

## Signals of a Strong Portfolio

- Range: at least two different types of PM work represented
- Honesty: at least one case study includes something that didn't go perfectly
- Ownership: every case study names at least 2-3 areas of explicit personal ownership
- Causality: every case study has at least one insight → decision → outcome chain
- Specificity: company names, user types, specific features, real numbers or real signals
- Voice: reads like a real person who did this work, not a template filler
